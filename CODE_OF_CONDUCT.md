# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

We want the ContextForge community to be professional, constructive, and welcoming.

Anyone participating in the ContextForge repository—including issues, feature requests, discussions, and other community interactions—is expected to communicate respectfully.

## Expected Behavior

Please:

- be respectful and constructive
- provide technical feedback clearly
- focus criticism on ideas, behavior, or software—not individuals
- respect different experience levels and perspectives
- avoid spam, harassment, threats, or discriminatory language
- avoid intentionally disruptive behavior

## Technical Discussions

Disagreement is welcome when it helps improve ContextForge.

When reporting bugs or suggesting changes, please provide enough technical information for others to understand and reproduce the issue.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior may result in moderation:

- harassment or personal attacks
- discriminatory or hateful language
- threats or intimidation
- publishing another person's private information
- deliberate spam or disruption
- abusive or intentionally misleading issue reports

## Enforcement

Repository maintainers may edit, hide, lock, or remove content that violates these expectations.

Repeated or serious violations may result in restrictions from participating in the repository.

## Security Issues

Security vulnerabilities should not be reported publicly.

Please follow the instructions in `SECURITY.md`.

## Contact

For normal bugs and feature requests, use the repository's GitHub Issues page.

Thank you for helping keep the ContextForge community constructive and professional.
